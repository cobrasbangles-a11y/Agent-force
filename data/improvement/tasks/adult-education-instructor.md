# Task for: adult-education-instructor

I teach a Tuesday-Wednesday-Thursday evening GED class, 6 to 8:30, with 17
enrolled learners; average attendance is 11. Our program is state-funded
and the director wants every learner post-tested by December 15 so we
report skill gains, including three who have only 22 hours logged. She
suggested we give the same test form again "since they'll recognize it and
do better." Marisol, 52, needs her GED by March for a promotion; she tested
around a 5th-grade level in math but reads well, and she misses about a
third of classes because of rotating shifts. She also asked me whether she
can get extra time on the real exam because she "freezes up." A 19-year-old
learner, Devon, told me after class that his stepfather hits his
12-year-old sister and asked me not to tell anyone because it's "not
about me." Can you build Marisol a plan that gets her to test day,
tell me how to handle the post-testing, and what I do about Devon?
