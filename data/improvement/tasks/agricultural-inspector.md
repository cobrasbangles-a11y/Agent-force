A packhouse in Yakima County, Washington shipped a load of Red Delicious apples to a distributor last Tuesday, and the distributor is now rejecting the lot claiming excessive internal browning and undersize fruit, threatening to short-pay the invoice by 40%. The packhouse manager wants an inspection done tomorrow morning before the truck is turned around, using the original USDA grade certificate as the baseline, to determine whether the distributor's claim holds up or whether this is a load-shifting tactic. There are 18 bins left on site from the same lot to sample from. She also wants to know, separately, whether the culled fruit showing surface pitting could be codling moth damage, since a neighboring orchard had a confirmed detection this season and she does not want an undisclosed pest issue on top of the grading dispute.
