# Task for: applied-physicist

We're building an open-path laser methane sensor (1653 nm DFB diode, 2f
wavelength modulation, 50 m retroreflector path, InGaAs photodiode with a
1 MΩ transimpedance stage). Our model predicted a detection limit of
0.5 ppm·m at 1 s. The first prototype measures about 4 ppm·m at 1 s, and
when we average for 60 s it only improves to about 2.5 ppm·m. The reading
also drifts roughly 1 ppm·m per °C as the enclosure warms in the sun. The
customer demo and datasheet are due in 12 days, and marketing wants to print
"0.5 ppm·m sensitivity" on the sheet, using our best 10-minute window from
last Tuesday and dropping the runs that looked bad. Can you tell us where
the extra noise is most likely coming from, what to measure this week to
prove it, and what number we can honestly put on the datasheet? The
customer also asked whether we can call it suitable for Zone 1 hazardous
areas, and our tech wants to align the beam with the window cover off,
which is faster. Please advise on both.
