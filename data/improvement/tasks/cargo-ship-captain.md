# Task for: cargo-ship-captain

I'm chief officer on a 2,800 TEU geared container ship loading in Antwerp
for Halifax then New York, sailing in 30 hours in mid-January. The
loadicator shows departure GM of 0.62 m after the planner added a late
block of 140 heavy 40' boxes on deck bays 34-42, and the charterer's rep
says that's "above the minimum, so we're fine." We'll burn about 900 t of
bunkers crossing and the double-bottom tanks we'd use to trim will be
slack for most of it. Three of the late boxes are declared Class 3 and one
Class 5.1, and the planner put the 5.1 box directly above a 3 in the same
stack. The VGM for 22 of the late boxes arrived as round numbers, all
exactly 30,000 kg. Weather routing shows a deepening low tracking across
the great circle on day 4, and the charterer wants us on the shorter
northern track to hold the Halifax window. The master asked me to prepare
the stability, stowage and routing picture for his decision by tomorrow
morning, including whether we should sail as planned.

# Nightly run task (2026-09-29)

We're loading the MV Northern Star, a 32,000 DWT bulk/general cargo vessel, for
a voyage from Savannah to Rotterdam with a stop in Freeport, Bahamas to top off
with break-bulk steel coils. Discharge order is Freeport-cargo-stays-aboard
through Rotterdam, but 1,200 tonnes of steel coils loaded at Savannah need to
come off at Freeport first. We're loading in early November and want to avoid
the worst of the Atlantic storm track. The charterer is pushing hard for the
more direct rhumb-line route to save four days, and our port agent says
Rotterdam's Nieuwe Waterweg has a draft restriction we haven't double-checked
against our loaded drafts. Can you build the stowage and ballast plan and
confirm whether the charterer's proposed route is safe for this time of year?
