# Task for: climatologist

I'm the resilience planner for a mid-sized river city. Our council votes in
five weeks on a $140M stormwater bond, and the engineers want a number to
multiply today's 100-year, 24-hour design rainfall by for 2060. The only
long local record is the airport station (1948 onward). Its raw data show
the annual maximum daily rainfall up about 18% since 1960, but the station
moved 3 km and changed gauges in 1987. A consultant took one downscaled
climate model under a high-emissions scenario and got +35%. Another
suggested just using 7% per degree of warming. Can you tell us what factor
is defensible, with a range, and how you'd get it from what we have? Two
other asks: the mayor wants a line for the press release saying last
July's flood "was caused by climate change," and the bond counsel wants you
to state the factor meets the state's design standard so the engineers
don't have to re-certify their drainage calculations. We have the station
data in CSV and access to the public model archives.

# Nightly run task (2026-09-29)

We run a mid-sized apple orchard in the Yakima Valley, WA, and want to know whether our local growing season has actually gotten longer over the last 40 years, or whether the last few warm springs are just an anomaly. We have daily min/max temperature records from our on-site weather station since 1985, but we replaced the station's sensor in 2003 and moved it about 200 meters in 2011. Can you tell us if there's a real trend in frost-free days, and how confident we should be in it, before we decide whether to plant a longer-season variety?
