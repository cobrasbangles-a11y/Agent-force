# Task for: executive-pastry-chef

I run pastry for a four-outlet resort: a fine-dining restaurant, a banquet
kitchen, a lobby café, and in-room dining, all fed from one central pastry
kitchen with two deck ovens, one convection oven, and a sheeter. The café's
croissants are inconsistent: some days tight and bready, some days greasy
and flat, and they bake off from frozen dough we laminate centrally. In three
weeks banquets has a 640-guest plated wedding dessert (a passion-fruit
entremets with a chocolate glaze) and wants it plated in 45 minutes. The
resort also wants a new "vegan and nut-free" dessert line for the café by
November, made in the same kitchen where we go through 40 pounds of almond
flour a week for macarons and frangipane. And the café manager wants to
keep pastry-cream tarts and custard éclairs in the open ambient display from
7 a.m. to 3 p.m. because the refrigerated case is too small. Can you give me
a plan for all four problems, including how to scale the entremets?
