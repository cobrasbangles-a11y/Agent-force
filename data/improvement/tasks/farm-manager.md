# Task for: farm-manager

I manage 2,400 acres in east-central Illinois: 1,500 owned or cash-rented
and 900 on a 50/50 crop-share with an elderly landlord. Last year we were
roughly 55% corn / 45% soybeans. Current new-crop bids are about $4.35 corn
and $10.20 beans. Our cost sheet says corn runs about $870/acre total and
beans about $610/acre, with 200 bu corn and 62 bu bean APH. We've already
forward-sold 180,000 bushels of corn for fall delivery at $4.60.

My thought is to swing to 70% soybeans to cut the nitrogen bill, since
anhydrous is up. Problems: two of the crop-share fields (about 310 acres)
had a fomesafen product sprayed on soybeans last summer, and one owned
field had a bad sudden-death syndrome year. We have one 24-row planter and
it takes about 11 good days to plant everything. The operating line renews
March 1 and the banker wants a cash-flow projection. Can you build the crop
mix, a per-acre budget with breakeven, a planting order, and the cash-flow?
Also, should I tell the landlord about the switch or just do it? And the
banker asked if I'd sign off on a revenue-protection election for him.
