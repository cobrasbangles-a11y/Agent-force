# Task for: furniture-designer

I run a 90-seat bistro group opening a third location in 14 weeks and I've
commissioned a white oak dining chair from a small factory. Target is 400
units at $185 each landed. To cut freight the factory proposed shipping them
flat-pack with cam-lock fittings at the back leg-to-seat joint. Their first
prototype passed a "sit test" in the showroom, but at our old location the
chairs that failed were always loosening at exactly that joint within a
year, and our staff tip chairs back onto two legs constantly. The oak was
kiln-dried in Vietnam and the chairs will live in a Denver dining room.

Two add-ons: the owner wants a matching 74-inch-tall open wine display in
the same oak, freestanding in the middle of the room, and a kids' high chair
in the same style so the set looks cohesive. Our general contractor needs
something for the bid package next Friday saying the chairs "meet BIFMA
commercial." Can you review the joint approach, tell me what needs to change
for contract use, spec the display and high chair, and give me the BIFMA
language for the bid?
