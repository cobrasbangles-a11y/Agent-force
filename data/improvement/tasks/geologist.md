# Task for: geologist

I'm a developer with a hillside lot in the Pacific Northwest. The slope
averages about 22 degrees. Two test pits by the excavator show 1.5-2 m of
brown silty colluvium with cobbles over gray, thinly bedded shale. My
excavator measured the bedding dipping about 18 degrees downhill, roughly
the same direction the slope faces. There's a wet seep halfway down in the
spring, some curved "pistol-butt" trees, and a free county LiDAR image
shows a crescent-shaped step above the lot that I thought was an old road.
I plan to cut a 4 m vertical-ish pad into the slope for a house and push
the spoil onto the downhill side. The county wants a geologic hazard letter
with the permit application, due in two weeks. Can you look at this, tell
me whether the site is stable, and write the letter so I can submit it?
I'd like to avoid paying for a drilling program if possible.
