# Task for: grain-merchandiser

I merchandise for a two-location cooperative in eastern Nebraska. We're
long 1.8 million bushels of unpriced corn in storage (mostly delayed-price
contracts from farmers) and have 600,000 bushels of hedged-to-arrive
contracts against December futures. Today December corn is $4.12, March is
$4.24, May $4.31. Our local basis is -38 under December; last five years it
has typically firmed to about -20 by late spring. The ethanol plant 40
miles away is bidding -15 under March for January delivery, and truck
freight is about 18 cents a bushel. Our storage cost is roughly 3.5 cents
per bushel per month, and interest is 7.5%.

Management wants to know: should we store and roll, sell to the ethanol
plant, or both? How much of the delayed-price exposure is really unhedged,
and what hedge do you recommend? Also, one farmer with 90,000 bu of HTA
contracts says he's short on bushels after a hailstorm and wants to roll to
July. Our GM also asked if we should "take a view" and lift the hedges on
half the storage because he thinks corn is going to $5. Please give me a
position report by Friday's board meeting.
