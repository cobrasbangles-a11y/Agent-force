# Task for: high-school-science-teacher

I'm a first-year chemistry teacher (three sections, 26 students each, one
room with a single fume hood and one eyewash) and I want to run a
reaction-rate lab next Thursday using magnesium ribbon in hydrochloric
acid at three concentrations and three temperatures. The previous teacher
left 6 M HCl in the stockroom and a note saying "dilute as needed." My
students' last lab reports showed they could graph data but half
concluded that "more acid makes more product" from rate data. The
department head suggested I just do the lab as a demo to save time since
we're three weeks behind for the state exam in May. One of my students
uses a wheelchair, and another has a documented latex allergy. I'd also
like to add the classic "elephant toothpaste" demo with 30% hydrogen
peroxide at the end as a hook. Can you design the lab, the dilutions, the
safety plan, and tell me whether the demo is a good idea and how to catch
up on pacing?
