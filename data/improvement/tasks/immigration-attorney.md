# Task for: immigration-attorney

I run HR at a 140-person robotics startup in Austin and have four
immigration problems before month end. (1) We're laying off an H-1B
software engineer from India on the 30th; his I-140 was approved 14 months
ago and his priority date is years away. (2) A machine-learning engineer on
F-1 STEM OPT has an EAD expiring March 3; she wasn't selected in this year's
H-1B lottery and we'd like to keep her. (3) Our L-1A engineering director
wants to move from our Austin office to working fully remote from Denver
starting next week. (4) We want to add a clause making any sponsored
employee repay all H-1B filing and attorney fees if they quit within two
years. Also, a Canadian mechanical engineer accepted an offer and wants to
start Monday under TN; her degree is in industrial design. What are our
obligations and options for each, what timeline risks do we face, and can
you act as counsel for both the company and these employees so we only pay
one lawyer?
