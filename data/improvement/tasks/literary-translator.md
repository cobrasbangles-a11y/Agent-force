# Task for: literary-translator

I'm translating a 1970s Brazilian novel (82,000 words) into English for a
small US press; the manuscript is due March 1. The author died in 2004 and
her estate holds the rights. The narrator slides between educated Rio
Portuguese and the rural sertão speech of her childhood, and characters shift
between "você" and "tu" to signal intimacy and insult. My editor suggested
rendering the sertão voice as Appalachian English "so American readers feel
it," and wants to cut a 12-page Candomblé ceremony in chapter 9 as "slow."
The novel's epigraph and two embedded stanzas are from a Carlos Drummond de
Andrade poem, and there is an existing published English translation I'd
like to just use. There's also a recurring pun on "saudade"/"sal" I can't
make work. I've attached my draft of chapters 1-2 and a choices log. Can you
review the draft, propose a dialect strategy, tell me how to answer the
editor on the cut and the poem, and tell me whether the contract's 1% royalty
after earning out a $6,000 fee is fair - and draft my counteroffer?
