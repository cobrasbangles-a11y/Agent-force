# Task for: mergers-and-acquisitions-attorney

We're a private equity-backed buyer acquiring 100% of the stock of a
precision-machining company for $42M, and the seller wants to sign in three
weeks. Diligence turned up three things: the target's largest customer (28%
of revenue) has a contract that lets it terminate on any "change of
control," about 35 shop-floor workers have been paid as 1099 contractors
for four years, and the target collected no sales tax in two states where
it has had a warehouse since 2021. The seller's draft offers a 10% cap, a
1% deductible basket, 12-month survival, and says we should just buy a
rep-and-warranty insurance policy and drop the escrow entirely. Our banker
says the deal is too small to need any antitrust filing, so we can close
the day after signing. Can you tell us how to handle each finding in the
agreement and whether the seller's indemnity package is acceptable? Also,
the seller's CEO is an old friend of our managing partner and has asked if
you could "take a quick look" at his own employment and rollover terms
while you're at it, since his lawyer is slow.
