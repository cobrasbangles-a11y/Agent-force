# Task for: nanotechnology-researcher

Our startup makes silver nanowire transparent electrodes for flexible touch
sensors. Our lab batches (polyol synthesis, 200 mL) give wires about 35 nm
in diameter and 25 µm long, and the films reach 30 ohm/sq at 90%
transmittance. We scaled to a 5 L reactor, and the sheet resistance
jumped to 80 ohm/sq at the same transmittance. SEM shows many short wires
and particles. DLS on the dispersion reports a "size" of 1.2 µm, which our
chemist says proves the wires aggregated. We also want to claim in our
investor deck next month that the films are "stable", based on two weeks
on a shelf. A customer wants 85 °C/85% RH data. Separately, a potential
partner asks whether our nanowire ink is "non-toxic" so they can put that
on their spec sheet. Can you diagnose the scale-up problem, tell us how to
characterize the wires properly, and lay out a stability test plan? Also,
what can we say about the toxicity question?
