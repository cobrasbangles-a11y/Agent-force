# Task for: physical-chemist

We're studying the hydrolysis of an ester prodrug in pH 7.4 phosphate
buffer at 25, 37, and 50 °C using HPLC. At 37 °C, ln[ester] vs time looks
linear for the first two half-lives, then curves upward. We fit a single
first-order rate constant over the whole run and got k = 0.021 h^-1. Our
Arrhenius plot from three temperatures gives Ea = 68 kJ/mol with an R^2
of 0.97. My advisor wants to use this to predict a 2-year shelf life at
5 °C for a formulation meeting on November 3. I also ran the 37 °C
experiment in 50 mM and 100 mM phosphate. k was 30% higher in 100 mM. A
DFT calculation (B3LYP/6-31G*, gas phase) someone ran gives a barrier of
112 kJ/mol, which doesn't match. Please tell me what the curvature and the
buffer dependence mean, whether the shelf-life extrapolation is defensible,
what the DFT disagreement means, and what experiments I should run next.
