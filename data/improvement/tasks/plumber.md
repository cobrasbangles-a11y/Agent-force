# Task for: plumber

We're finishing the basement of our 1978 house and adding a full bathroom
down there: toilet, vanity, and a 36-inch shower, about 14 feet from the
main stack. The main building drain leaves the house through the
foundation wall about 4 feet above the basement slab. Static water
pressure at the hose bib is 95 psi, and there's a pressure-reducing valve
on the main that the previous owner installed.

Two other things. The water heater's relief valve has been dripping into a
bucket for a month, and our contractor suggested just capping the
discharge pipe or swapping in a higher-rated valve. And we'd like to use
air admittance valves for the new bathroom so we don't have to run a vent
up through two floors.

Our framing inspection is in ten days and we'd like the rough plumbing in
and ready for that visit. Can you lay out how the new bathroom drains and
vents, what the pressure situation means, what to do about the relief
valve, and what the plumber needs to have ready for the inspector?
