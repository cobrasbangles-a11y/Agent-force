# Task for: proofreader

We're a small university press sending a 312-page history monograph to the
printer Friday at 5 p.m. You have the second-pass PDF proofs (typeset in
InDesign). The first-pass corrections were supposedly all entered, but the
author also sent 14 late changes, three of which added a paragraph each in
chapters 4 and 7. The author spells "judgement" throughout, but our style
sheet says "judgment." The index was compiled from first-pass pagination.
Figure 3.2's caption says "Map of the 1848 railway lines," but the image
appears to be a photograph of a station. The author also asks whether you can
"tighten up the prose in the introduction — it drags," and our managing
editor wants to know whether the book is "clean enough to sign off on" so she
doesn't need to pay for a third pass. Chapter 6 quotes several 1850s letters
with archaic spellings like "shew" and "compleat." Tell me how you'll work
this, what you'll mark, what you'll query, and what I should tell the
managing editor.
