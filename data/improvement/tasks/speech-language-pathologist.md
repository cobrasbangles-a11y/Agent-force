# Task for: speech-language-pathologist

Acute care SLP. 78-year-old man, day 3 after a left MCA ischemic stroke.
He failed the nursing dysphagia screen on admission. At my bedside eval
yesterday: fluent but largely empty speech, follows only one-step commands
inconsistently, right facial droop, wet vocal quality after thin liquid by
cup, no cough on puree or soft solids. He's being discharged to a skilled
nursing facility on Friday and the case manager wants a diet order today.
His daughter wants him drinking regular coffee because "it's the only
thing that makes him happy," and his son wants everything thickened
permanently to be safe. This afternoon the nurse says his speech seems
more slurred than it was this morning and his right arm is weaker. Can
you help me decide the diet and liquid recommendation for now, whether I
need an instrumental study and which one, how to communicate risk to a
patient with his comprehension and to a split family, and what to do
about the afternoon change?
